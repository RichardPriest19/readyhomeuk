Emergency water and food: how much to store

Last updated October 2026

Water is the single most important thing in any emergency kit. Mains supplies can be cut off by burst pipes, flooding, freezing weather or contamination, and you may be told to boil water at a time when you have no power to do it.

The UK government recommends 2.5 to 3 litres of drinking water per person per day as a minimum, or about 10 litres per person per day to cover cooking and washing too. Plan for at least three days.

Sealed bottled water from the supermarket is the cheapest and safest option. Buy a few extra packs and use them in normal life, replacing them as you go, so your stock never goes out of date. A food-grade container lets you store extra tap water for washing and flushing when an outage is announced.

Food that needs no cooking

Choose food you can eat cold, straight from the tin or packet: ring-pull tins, ready meals, oat bars, nuts and dried fruit. Pick things your household actually likes so you can rotate them into normal meals. Don't forget a manual tin opener.

  3. Water purification tablets and filter

    Make water safe to drink when there's a 'boil water' notice and no power to boil it.

    For one person: 1 pack of tablets (treats 2,000 litres), and a filter for each grab bag
    For a family of four: 1 pack of tablets (treats 2,000 litres), and a filter for each grab bag

    If your water supplier issues a 'boil water' notice during a power cut, you may have no way to boil water. Purification tablets make clear water safe to drink, and a personal filter lets you drink safely on the move. They're a back-up to bottled water, not a replacement for it.

    • Tablets that say how many litres each one treats
    • A personal filter for each grab bag
    • Check the expiry date: tablets usually last about 5 years

  7. Vacuum flask

    Fill it with boiling water when a power cut is forecast, and you'll have hot drinks for a day.

    For one person: 1 × 1-litre flask
    For a family of four: 2 × 1-litre flask

    A good vacuum flask keeps water hot for around 24 hours. When a storm or planned power cut is on the way, fill it from the kettle. You'll have hot drinks, instant soup or porridge without any power.

    • Stainless steel, double-walled, at least 1 litre
    • A pouring stopper that doesn't need to be unscrewed
    • Warm it with hot water first, then refill with boiling water

  8. Emergency toilet liners

    When the water is off, toilets can't flush. Liners turn your own toilet into a sealed, hygienic one.

    For one person: 6 liners for 3 days (1 × 20-pack)
    For a family of four: 24 liners for 3 days (2 × 20-pack)

    If the mains water is cut off, you can usually flush only once with the water left in the cistern. Disposable liners with absorbent pads fit over your own toilet bowl and seal waste in a bag, so you don't waste drinking water on flushing. Tie up used liners and store them outside until bin collections resume.

    • Liners with absorbent or gelling pads, which turn waste solid and cut smells
    • A universal fit for a standard toilet bowl
    • Strong bin bags and somewhere outside to keep used liners

Frequently asked questions

How much water does one person need per day?

At least 2.5 to 3 litres just to drink. To cover cooking and washing as well, plan on about 10 litres per person per day.

Does bottled water go off?

Sealed bottled water keeps for a long time, but the best-before date is a good guide to quality. Store it somewhere cool and dark, and rotate it through normal use before the date.

Can I store tap water?

Yes, in clean, food-grade containers with a lid. Store it somewhere cool and dark and replace it regularly, roughly every six months.

What food is best for an emergency kit?

Food that needs no cooking and keeps for months: ring-pull tins, long-life ready meals, oat and cereal bars, nuts, dried fruit and emergency ration bars.
