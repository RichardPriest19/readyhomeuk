Power cut kit: what every UK home needs

Last updated October 2026

Most power cuts in the UK are fixed within a few hours. But after big storms, some homes have been without power for several days. When the electricity goes, so does more than the lights: most central heating stops, fridges warm up, phones run flat and, if your landline has moved to Digital Voice, your home phone stops working too.

In England, Scotland and Wales you can call 105 for free to report a power cut and get updates. Below is everything worth keeping ready, starting with the items on the government's own checklist.

Safety first

Use LED torches and lanterns rather than candles, which cause house fires every year. Never use a barbecue, camping stove, patio heater or petrol generator indoors: they give off carbon monoxide, which can kill. A battery power station is the safe way to run appliances indoors.

What to keep ready

  1. Wind-up or battery torch

    Light that works when the mains is off. Far safer than candles. On the government's list.

    For one person: 1 torch, one per adult, or 1 head torch 2-pack
    For a family of four: 2 torches, one per adult, or 1 head torch 2-pack

    The government's Prepare campaign lists a battery or wind-up torch first, because candles are a leading cause of house fires during power cuts. A wind-up model never runs out of batteries.

    • A hand-crank or USB-rechargeable LED torch, ideally with both
    • At least 100 lumens for moving around the house
    • A head torch as well, so your hands are free

  2. Rechargeable LED lantern

    Lights a whole room, not just a path. Far safer than candles.

    For one person: 1 per room you'll use in the evening
    For a family of four: 1 per room you'll use in the evening

    A torch is good for moving around, but a lantern lights a room so you can cook, read and look after children in a power cut. Candles cause house fires every year, especially during power cuts, so an LED lantern is the safer choice.

    • Rechargeable, with a long runtime on a low setting
    • A warm light setting is easier on the eyes in the evening
    • Can also charge a phone, as a bonus

  3. Spare batteries

    For torches, radios, smoke alarms and any medical equipment. On the government's list.

    For one person: A multipack of AA and AAA
    For a family of four: A multipack of AA and AAA

    The guidance asks you to keep spare batteries for torches, radios and medical devices. Alkaline batteries keep for up to ten years in a drawer.

    • Check which sizes your torch, radio and alarms take (usually AA and AAA)
    • Long-life alkaline for storage, not rechargeables that slowly drain
    • Write the expiry year on the pack

  4. Portable power bank

    Keeps phones charged so you can get alerts and call for help. On the government's list.

    For one person: 1 power bank of 20,000 mAh
    For a family of four: 1 power bank of 20,000 mAh

    Your phone receives the government's Emergency Alerts and is how you'll contact family. A charged power bank gives several full phone charges during a long outage.

    • 20,000 mAh or more for several phone charges
    • The right cables for every phone in the house
    • Keep it topped up: check and recharge every three months
    • A car charger lets you charge phones from the car during a power cut

    Hear official updates even when the internet and mobile networks are down. On the government's list.

    For a family of four: 1 per household

    Local and national radio broadcast emergency updates during power cuts. The government recommends a battery or wind-up radio so you are not relying on mobile data.

    • Hand-crank and solar charging, with batteries as back-up
    • FM/AM is enough for BBC local radio and emergency updates
    • A built-in torch and USB phone-charging port are useful extras

  6. Hand warmers and sleeping bags

    Most central heating stops in a power cut. Stay warm without it.

    For one person: 1 sleeping bag, plus 1 box of 40 pairs of hand warmers
    For a family of four: 4 sleeping bags, plus 1 box of 40 pairs of hand warmers

    Gas boilers need electricity to run, so most homes lose their heating in a power cut. In winter, a warm sleeping bag for each person and disposable hand warmers keep you comfortable through a cold night. Older people and young children feel the cold fastest.

    • A 3-season or warmer sleeping bag for each adult and child
    • Air-activated hand warmers last up to 10 hours each
    • Never use hand warmers on babies, and don't put them directly on skin

  7. Vacuum flask

    Fill it with boiling water when a power cut is forecast, and you'll have hot drinks for a day.

    For one person: 1 × 1-litre flask
    For a family of four: 2 × 1-litre flask

    A good vacuum flask keeps water hot for around 24 hours. When a storm or planned power cut is on the way, fill it from the kettle. You'll have hot drinks, instant soup or porridge without any power.

    • Stainless steel, double-walled, at least 1 litre
    • A pouring stopper that doesn't need to be unscrewed
    • Warm it with hot water first, then refill with boiling water

  8. Portable power station

    A big rechargeable battery with UK plug sockets. Keeps phones, lights and Wi-Fi going for a day or two.

    For one person: 1 per household
    For a family of four: 1 per household

    A portable power station is like a giant power bank with normal three-pin sockets. A 250–500Wh model can run your broadband router for a day or more, recharge every phone in the house many times, and power lamps and a laptop. Unlike a petrol generator it gives off no fumes, so it's safe to use indoors.

    • Capacity in watt-hours (Wh): 250–500Wh covers phones, lights and Wi-Fi
    • LiFePO4 (LFP) batteries last for thousands of charges
    • Top it up every 3 months so it's full when you need it

  9. Home backup power station

    Around 1–2kWh of stored power: enough to keep a fridge or medical equipment running through a long power cut.

    For one person: 1 per household, if you need to run a fridge or medical equipment
    For a family of four: 1 per household, if you need to run a fridge or medical equipment

    A larger power station stores enough energy for essential appliances. As a rough guide, 1,000Wh can keep a fridge-freezer going for most of a day, or run a CPAP machine for several nights. Many switch over within milliseconds when the power fails, so they can protect the router or medical equipment automatically. Pair one with a solar panel to recharge it during a long outage.

    • 1,000Wh or more for a fridge; check the wattage of anything you need to run
    • UPS or pass-through mode, to switch over automatically when the power fails
    • If someone at home relies on powered medical equipment, also join the Priority Services Register

  10. Battery backup for your broadband router

    Keeps your Wi-Fi and home phone working in a power cut.

    For one person: 1 per household
    For a family of four: 1 per household

    UK landlines are moving from copper lines to internet calling (Digital Voice), with the switch-over due to finish by early 2027. Once your line has switched, your home phone stops working in a power cut because the router has no power. A small battery backup keeps the router, Wi-Fi and home phone running for several hours. Providers only have to offer one to customers who depend on their landline, for example people with a telecare alarm or no mobile signal.

    • Check your router's power socket: most UK broadband hubs take 12V
    • Enough capacity for several hours, at least 10,000 mAh
    • An old corded phone won't help once your line has switched to Digital Voice

  12. Long-life food

    Food you can eat without cooking: tins, pouches and snacks. On the government's list.

    For one person: 9 meals for 3 days: 1 pack of 12 tins, plus snacks
    For a family of four: 36 meals for 3 days: 3 packs of 12 tins, plus snacks

    The guidance recommends non-perishable food that doesn't need cooking, like tinned meat and vegetables, because you may have no power to cook with.

    • Ring-pull tins, so you don't even need an opener
    • Ready-to-eat pouches, oat bars, nuts and dried fruit
    • Food your household actually likes, so you can rotate it into normal meals

Frequently asked questions

What number do I call in a power cut?

Call 105. It's free from most phones in England, Scotland and Wales and puts you through to your local electricity network operator. In Northern Ireland, contact NIE Networks.

How long do power cuts last in the UK?

Most are fixed within a few hours. After severe storms, damage to overhead lines can leave some homes without power for several days, which is why the government suggests keeping supplies for at least three days.

Will my landline work in a power cut?

Older copper landlines usually did. Most UK lines are moving to Digital Voice, which runs through your broadband router and stops working when the power goes, unless the router has a battery backup.

Is it safe to use a portable power station indoors?

Yes. Battery power stations give off no fumes, unlike petrol generators. Keep them dry, ventilated and away from heat, as you would any large battery.
