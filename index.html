Where this list comes from

The core items come from the UK government's Prepare campaign, which asks households to keep a torch, radio, power bank, spare batteries, first aid kit, hand sanitiser and wipes, bottled water, food that needs no cooking, and enough medication for several days.

Water amounts follow the same guidance: 2.5 to 3 litres per person per day just to drink, or 10 litres per person per day to cover cooking and washing too.

FAQ

Emergency kit questions

What should be in an emergency kit in the UK?

The UK government's Prepare campaign asks every household to keep a battery or wind-up torch, a battery or wind-up radio, a portable power bank, spare batteries, a first aid kit, hand sanitiser and wet wipes, bottled water, food that needs no cooking (with a tin opener) and enough of any regular medication to last several days.

Families should also plan for babies (ready-to-feed formula) and pets. Our 10 essentials list covers the core items.

How much water should I store for an emergency?

Government guidance is 2.5 to 3 litres per person per day just to drink, or about 10 litres per person per day to cover cooking and washing too.

For three days that's 9 to 30 litres per person, or 36 to 120 litres for a family of four. See our water and food storage guide for a table by household size.

How many days of supplies should I keep?

Aim for at least three days. Most emergencies are over sooner, but after major storms and floods some homes have waited several days for power or water to come back.

What should I do in a power cut?

Check whether your neighbours are affected, then call 105 (free, in England, Scotland and Wales) to report it and get updates. Switch off appliances except one light, keep the fridge and freezer closed, and check on vulnerable neighbours.

Never use a barbecue, camping stove or generator indoors, because of the risk of carbon monoxide poisoning. Our power cut kit guide covers what to keep ready.

Where should I keep my emergency kit?

Keep it together in one place that's easy to reach and that everyone at home knows about. Avoid the loft, and avoid the garage or cellar if your home could flood. Keep a packed grab bag near the door, and check dates and batteries every six months.
